Advanced Course: Bitcoin Business Operations

BTCPay Server, staff workflows, online checkout, and over-the-counter payments

Overview

What this guide teaches:

This course covers the day-to-day side of accepting Bitcoin in a small business. It is about the payment flow that customers and employees touch, not the deepest long-term seed storage layer.

The goal is to keep Bitcoin practical at the counter and online while preventing staff devices, checkout screens, and hot wallets from becoming the business vault.

You will structure BTCPay Server, over-the-counter payments, online checkout, staff permissions, operating liquidity, sweeps, outage handling, and incident response.

Stage 1: Operating Layers

Stage 1.1: Counter, online, and reserve roles

A business payment setup works best when each wallet layer has one job. The counter flow should accept payments quickly. The online flow should handle remote orders and invoices. The reserve layer should hold funds that do not need to remain online.

Do not run the shop from the same wallet that protects long-term savings. Treat the checkout system like a cash register: useful, visible, and limited.

Stage 1.2: Staff boundaries

Employees should be able to create invoices and confirm payment status without seeing seed words, wallet backups, node credentials, or owner reserve controls.

A BTCPay Server Point of Sale page is a cleaner boundary than a normal mobile wallet at the cash register. Staff can request payment and see whether it arrived, while owners keep control of wallet recovery, Lightning liquidity, sweeps, and treasury movement.

Use these actor categories when drawing process maps: Owner A, Owner B, Keyholder C, Employee, and Customer. In this operations course, Keyholder C usually does not participate in day-to-day payments. Keyholder C belongs to the deep reserve recovery process, not the cash register.

Good operating boundary

  • Staff can create invoices and verify paid invoices
  • Owners manage wallet backups and reserve transfers
  • Counter devices hold limited authority
  • Online checkout does not expose deep storage keys

Weak operating boundary

  • The cash register phone also controls reserve funds
  • Employees can restore or spend from a business wallet
  • Website payment credentials are shared casually
  • No one knows the fallback process during an outage

Stage 2: BTCPay Server Setup

Stage 2.1: POS and online checkout

BTCPay Server can support both in-person and online payment flows. For the counter, use a Point of Sale app that staff can open on a tablet or register screen. For online payments, connect BTCPay Server to the website, webshop, invoice page, or payment link workflow.

The owner should control the store settings, wallet connection, Lightning setup, user roles, and sweep policy. Staff accounts should be limited to the screens they need for customer service.

Stage 2.2: Online and offline operations

Online checkout needs reliable server access, invoice creation, payment status checks, and order reconciliation. Over-the-counter payments need a fast local routine that still works when the shop is busy.

Prepare a fallback plan for connectivity problems. If the internet is down, staff should know whether the shop pauses Bitcoin payments, uses a pre-approved backup payment method, or asks the customer to wait until payment status can be verified. Avoid improvised wallet sharing during an outage.

Stage 3: Wallet Flow

Stage 3.1: Counter liquidity

The counter wallet or Lightning balance should hold only enough value to keep sales moving. It should have a refill threshold, a maximum balance, and a clear owner responsibility for replenishing liquidity.

Normal mobile Lightning wallets are better treated as owner or personal wallets, not employee cash register wallets. If a cashier can restore the wallet elsewhere, that cashier has too much authority over business funds.

Stage 3.2: Sweeps and refills

Revenue from the counter and online checkout should move through a planned flow. Keep operational liquidity online, sweep excess funds into an owner-controlled operating reserve, and move long-term retained earnings into deep storage on a schedule.

Make the thresholds visible to owners but not to casual staff. A simple weekly review is often enough for small shops: reconcile paid invoices, check liquidity, sweep excess, and document any refunds or exceptions.

Function Typical person Recommended permission level
Create payment invoices Cashier or owner Allowed through BTCPay Server POS or checkout only
Verify payment status Cashier or owner Allowed through checkout or POS view only
Refund customer Owner or trained manager Allowed through a documented approval flow
Refill Lightning liquidity Owner Allowed from operating reserve only
Sweep funds to deep storage Owner or both owners Never from a staff device

Stage 4: Staff and Incidents

Stage 4.1: Employee access

Staff onboarding should be specific and narrow. Show employees how to create an invoice, confirm paid status, handle an unpaid or expired invoice, and escalate refunds or unusual requests.

When staff leave, remove account access, rotate shared passwords where needed, review devices, and check whether any checkout links or permissions were exposed beyond their intended use.

Stage 4.2: Outages and compromise

Incident procedures should be written before the shop is busy. A stolen tablet, broken phone, failed Lightning channel, unavailable BTCPay Server instance, or suspicious refund request should trigger a known checklist instead of improvisation.

Operational event Required action
New cashier starts Limit access to invoice creation and payment status checks
Staff member leaves Review accounts, devices, passwords, checkout links, and POS access immediately
Counter device lost Remove device access, review payment credentials, and restore the POS flow safely
BTCPay Server outage Use the documented fallback plan and reconcile any delayed payments afterward
Counter wallet compromise Drain, isolate, investigate, and rebuild the operating payment flow

Stage 5: Summary

In this course you separated customer-facing Bitcoin operations from the deeper business reserve. Staff can accept payments and check status without receiving seed phrases, signer control, or reserve authority.

You now have the outline of a merchant payment system with BTCPay Server POS, online checkout, operating liquidity, sweep rules, employee access control, and incident procedures.
